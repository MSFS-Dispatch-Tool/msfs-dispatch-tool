DISPATCH TERMS

FLIGHT SEARCH

1L 1L
One-leg trip: a single sector from departure to destination
ML ML
Multi-leg trip: two sectors flown back to back on the same aircraft, out and back
DOM DOM
Domestic: both airports are in the same country
INT INT
International: the airports are in different countries
EBT
Expected block time of a trip: from the first off-block to the last in-block, ground time between legs included
TURNAROUND
Time on the ground between two legs. The minimum is 30 minutes: a late arrival that eats into it delays the next leg
FLIGHT CATEGORY
Weather at an airport by ceiling and visibility: VFR (good), MVFR (marginal), IFR and LIFR (low cloud or poor visibility)
SIGMET
Warning of significant weather for an area: thunderstorms, severe turbulence or icing, volcanic ash

SCHEDULE AND TIMES

SOBT / SIBT
Scheduled off-block and in-block times: when the aircraft is due to leave and reach the gate
STOT / SLDT
Scheduled take-off and landing times: SOBT plus taxi-out, SIBT minus taxi-in
EOBT / ETOT / ELDT / EIBT
Expected times: the scheduled ones moved by any delay. These are the times sent to SimBrief
AOBT / ATOT / ALDT / AIBT
Actual times you enter in the flight report: off-block, take-off, landing and in-block
EET
Estimated elapsed time: airborne time only, from take-off to landing
Z (ZULU)
Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). All times in the app are Zulu unless marked local

LOADS AND FUEL

PAX
Passengers: the seats on the assigned aircraft times a realistic load factor (about 82 to 99%, sometimes a full flight)
CARGO
Hold load from checked bags, based on the passenger count and sector length, with the occasional heavy-baggage flight
COST INDEX
How the flight management computer trades fuel against time: low is economical, high is fast. Set per leg and sent to SimBrief
ZFW / EZFW
Zero fuel weight: the aircraft with passengers, bags and cargo but no fuel. EZFW is the OFP estimate
TOW / ETOW
Take-off weight. ETOW is the OFP estimate
FOB / EFOB
Fuel on board. EFOB is the block fuel planned in the OFP
AFAD
Actual fuel at destination, entered in the flight report
LOADSHEET
The weight and balance document load control issues from the OFP: passengers, bags, fuel and weights against their limits. The captain signs it before departure
LMC
Last-minute change: a change to passengers or bags after the loadsheet is issued. The captain accepts or declines extra load; offloads are noted
OVERSOLD
More passengers booked than seats, as airlines plan on some not showing up. When everyone does, volunteers are moved to a later flight and offloaded with their bags
DG
Dangerous goods: items with a UN number and class, like dry ice or lithium batteries
NOTOC
Notification to captain: the list of dangerous goods in the hold, with where they are loaded. The captain signs it, or refuses the consignment
UNDERLOAD
How much more load the aircraft could take before reaching its most limiting weight (zero fuel, take-off or landing)

AIRCRAFT DEFECTS

MEL
Minimum equipment list: the official list of equipment an aircraft may fly without, and the conditions that apply. Each deferred defect has a deadline to be fixed
CDL
Configuration deviation list: external parts that may be missing, such as a panel or seal, usually with a small fuel or weight penalty
NOT DISPATCHABLE
The aircraft cannot operate the trip with its defects in today’s weather. Swap the aircraft or choose another flight
APU
Auxiliary power unit: the small turbine in the tail that supplies power and air on the ground and starts the engines
GPU / ASU
Ground power unit and air start unit: ground equipment that replaces the APU at the gate
FL
Flight level: altitude in hundreds of feet on the standard pressure setting. FL250 is 25,000 ft
CAT I / II / III
Approach categories: CAT I needs reasonable visibility; CAT II and III allow landings in fog and need more equipment working

DELAYS AND AIR TRAFFIC

DELAY CODE
The standard two-digit code airlines use to record why a flight left late (IATA codes). You choose one in the flight report
KNOCK-ON DELAY
A late inbound aircraft delaying the next leg (code 93). Expected from dispatch, then recalculated from your actual arrival
ATFM
Air traffic flow management: limits on how many flights can use an airspace or airport at once, which cause slot delays
SLOT (CTOT)
Calculated take-off time given by air traffic flow management. Take off between 5 minutes before and 10 minutes after it, or a new slot is issued
CURFEW
Hours when an airport is closed to departures or arrivals, usually at night. A delay that pushes a flight past it means a cancellation or a diversion
OTP
On-time performance: the share of flights that left (or arrived) within 15 minutes of schedule

CREW AND PAPERWORK

FDP
Flight duty period: from crew report, 45 minutes before the first departure, to the last in-block. Its maximum depends on report time and the number of sectors (EASA rules); the captain can extend it by up to 2 hours
OFP
Operational flight plan from SimBrief: route, fuel and weights for the leg
PIREP
Pilot report filed after landing with your actual times and fuel; it closes the leg and goes into your logbook

BEFORE YOU DISPATCH

This app doesn't tell SimBrief which aircraft to use. On the dispatch form's Aircraft Info section, set the Aircraft Type yourself — and the specific Variant or Airframe too, if you have one you'd rather fly — before generating the OFP

Reviewing the generated flight plan is your own responsibility. Only press Generate on SimBrief once you're satisfied the route, fuel, and weights all look right
